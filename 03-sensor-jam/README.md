Reflection on the Final Project - Kavipooja Selvakumar 
 
One of the most revolutionary creative-technical projects I have worked on is AstraBloom. The concept was straightforward when I first started, a touch that produces a visual bloom. However, as the project progressed, it organically transformed into a far more complex interactive experience that included visual storytelling, sensor-based triggers, and constellations.

The most significant change occurred when I added an Arduino pressure sensor to the system. All of a sudden, the interaction stopped being simulated and started to become real. I had to reconsider timing, thresholds, responsiveness, and user behavior in real space as a result. In order to give a consistent response in the system, I learnt how to apply refractory periods, smooth noisy sensor readings, and create predictable interaction rules.

The images changed dramatically as well. Simple floating circles were the earliest prototypes. I developed them into flowers, stars, constellation routes, and uncommon "Astra Orbs" by repetition. Every visual modification encouraged me to investigate state machines, event-driven design, easing functions, and p5.js animation. Additionally, I had to think about how to make the interface soothing, easy to use, and enchanting without overpowering the viewer.

The most important thing I learnt was how to combine software and hardware into a cohesive story. I worked on debugging two different systems, striking a balance between technological limitations and aesthetics, and making adjustments in response to user testing. Most significantly, I learned that even seemingly little interactions can have significance if they are purposefully created; this is a concept I will apply to my upcoming interactive projects.
